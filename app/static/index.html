Connecting

Every evening, Texas loses 30,000 MW of solar. We see each drop 15 minutes early.

Solar Shield forecasts ERCOT solar output on IBM LinuxONE and tells grid operators which batteries to dispatch so hospitals and people on home medical equipment keep their power.

    How a forecast becomes a decision

    These four steps run every 15 minutes. During a replay, the active step lights up.

    Read the grid

    ERCOT solar output and weather from five Texas solar regions.

    –

    Forecast on IBM Z

    Gradient boosting on LinuxONE compares today against a clear-sky curve.

    –

    Raise an alert

    A forecast drop of 400 MW or more triggers a ramp-down alert.

    –

    Protect people first

    Batteries near the most electricity-dependent patients are dispatched after operator approval.

    How well it forecasts

    Average forecast error in MW on August–September 2026, lower is better. Smart persistence assumes today stays as cloudy as it is now, which is the standard baseline for solar forecasting.

    Ramp-down alerts

    F1 balances catching real drops against false alarms. Higher is better.

    Who needs the power most

    Patient counts come from HHS emPOWER, aggregated by ZIP code. No personal data is used. When an operator approves an alert, the dispatched batteries light up and send power toward the highest-risk ZIP codes.
